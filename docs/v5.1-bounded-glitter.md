# v5.1 · Bounded glitter height and brush-required adhesion

The supplied final-background screenshot exposed an unbounded density-to-height conversion. A compressed cell could lift a mound vertex and falling particles far above the paper, producing a tall needle. This was a height artifact, not additional emitted particles.

Raw density remains available to the pressure solver. Physical height now uses a weighted 3×3 spatial filter and a saturating 32-unit maximum. Falling particles never snap upward to a surface above their previous height. Free particle and mound rendering have independent height bounds as a second guard. The continuous mound envelope only represents a dense pile; thinner scatter stays individual grains rather than a shiny connected film. Tests stress 18,000 packets at one coordinate, conserve the packet count, and verify bounded physical and rendered heights.

Adhesion now requires prior bristle contact, including the low-level binding operation. Settling alone cannot fill or advance a colour, even when a pour lands directly on a tiny target. The bottle aims 65–80 paper units above the adhesive anchor, with a narrower cone for small details. Small doses start at 360 packets instead of 1,600; large doses cap at 14,500 before the existing amount setting. The 95%/98% final-gap assistance remains after real brushing.

The mouth regression uses the actual stencil: it remains at zero coverage after a full pour and a prolonged wait, then gains coverage when the pile is brushed downward onto it. All colour pour plans, finite-material checks, trough collection, sequential peel and existing input regressions remain covered.
